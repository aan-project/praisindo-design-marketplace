Guide

Principles

The principles behind every decision of the system. Read them before the components: they say why a screen looks and behaves as it does.

  1. Attention → why → action → customer → resultEvery screen reads in this order. Lists open with what needs attention; details explain why before offering an action.
  2. One card is one boxContent inside a card is grouped with spacing, hairlines and leading icons, never with another bordered box.
  3. No unnecessary shadowsElevation is a border and a surface tone. Shadows are not used.
  4. Large interactions open in a drawerForms, filters, exports and comparisons use the right-side drawer (S 400, M 560, L 760 px), not modal dialogs.
  5. The system suggests, people decidePraisindo never sends messages or executes transactions automatically. Inferred values are labelled as estimates or demo data.
  6. Permissions live in the handlerHiding a button is not access control. Every action checks its permission when it runs.

Foundations

Motion

How the interface moves: between steps, to a target on the page and across the sign-in boundary. All motion is switched off when the system asks for reduced motion.

Motion for steps and jumps

Moving to another step: the content slides 14px in the direction of travel and fades in, one block after another. Go to: the page scrolls smoothly to the target, which gets a short outline highlight. Both are switched off when the system asks for reduced motion.

A brand-colour veil covers the screen when the user crosses the sign-in boundary: it wipes up from the bottom (0.42 s), the page changes underneath while the logo and a short status show, then it wipes away to the top (0.46 s). Used only after a successful sign-in and on sign-out, not between ordinary pages (they fade, see below). Clicks are blocked while it runs; with reduced motion the page changes at once without the veil, and the status is announced to screen readers.

Page fade

Between ordinary pages the content of the new page fades in over 0.2 s (token --dur-page); the sidebar and the top bar stay. Nothing slides and nothing is shown while the page loads. It stands still when the system asks for reduced motion and in automated browsers (tests and screenshots), so the screen is always final when it is looked at.

The numbers of the stat cards, the KPI and the widgets count up from 0 to their value in 0.6 s, once, when they appear (prop animate, on by default; animate={false} shows them at once). The decimals, the grouping and the currency of the text are kept and the last frame is exactly the text it was given; a later change of the value shows at once. Reduced motion and automated browsers show the final value at once.

Quick feedback and entrances

Small things answer the hand: a button presses in a little (scale 0.98) and its colours ease in 0.12 s; table cells and chips ease their colour. Things that open arrive softly in 0.2 s: a popover rises 4px while it fades in, a toast comes up 8px, the scrim and a centred dialog fade.   • No card inside a card: use list rows, hairlines, leading icons and spacing.

Status drawer

For readiness or health information in a drawer: a summary header with the overall status first, then a list of items that need attention, then one bordered card per source. Fields inside a card are rows with an icon, a name and a small value (bar, badge). Colour is only on icons and the summary, not on every row.

Calm drawers

A drawer should not stack alerts. Put the context in one muted line, group the cautions in a Worth knowing list (colour only on the icon) and write impact as a plain sentence with a small icon. Keep an Alert for one blocking message.

A drawer opened from another drawer shows an arrow Back button before its title and in the footer. Back returns to the previous drawer; Close closes everything. Example: Data readiness > Open data sources.
